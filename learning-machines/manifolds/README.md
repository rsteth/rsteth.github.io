# Learning Machines / 03 — website

Static article plus four independent, progressively enhanced panels. The site owns writing, DOM controls and the original orthographic 3D renderer. Numerical mechanics and synthetic fixtures come from the exact committed `rsteth/learning-machines` revision in `mechanics-pin.json`; no runtime remote requests are made.

## Update the pin

```sh
node scripts/sync-learning-machines.mjs /path/to/learning-machines FULL_COMMIT_SHA manifolds
node scripts/render-manifold-preview.mjs
node scripts/test-manifolds.mjs
node scripts/test-arrows.mjs
```

The optional `manifolds` selector preserves the previous attention-memory default sync command and does not alter studies 01–02. Sync reads committed Git blobs and writes hashes, MIT license, exact tree and commit URLs. The generated static previews use the same pinned FK, grid, route and PCA fixtures as the live panels. Do not copy working-tree mechanics into the page or use an invented revision.

## Illustration view

The shared live/static renderer uses a front-facing 180° camera yaw: physical +Y remains forward, while increasing forward distance appears screen-left/down. The visible torso/head faces are -X, +Y and the top. The screen-right/down/toward-camera basis preserves chirality; no joint coordinates, target poses, fixtures or PCA data are inverted.

## Controls and mathematical labels

- Free arms: fourteen actual joint coordinates within rig limits; ball stays unattached. Wrist axis markers show 3D orientation.
- Rigid grasp: six ball coordinates plus two posture preferences. Failure retains the last accepted pose and draws the requested target with a dashed outline. Release enables independent joint controls. Reset to canonical grasp loads a known example; it is not a simulated contact transition.
- Cost (`terrain.mjs`): the sampled y/z slice, with fixed x, orientation and posture, drawn as ground in a three-quarter view. Each valid sample is a point whose height and shade are its normalized cost, and there is no ground where a sample has no valid pose. Four-neighbour shortest and weighted least-cost routes run over it. Pointing at a route shows the robot at that step, clicking valid ground moves the goal, and a sideways drag or the left and right arrow keys spin it at a fixed pitch; the goal sliders remain the keyboard path. The formulas for the three costs and the route objective sit in a collapsed note in the panel. The no-JavaScript fallback is still the flat chart in `landscape-static.svg`.
- Skill threads (`threads.mjs`): each synthetic handoff is one thread on PC1 and PC2; the third axis is joint-space distance from the fitted two-component plane at the same scale. All scores are computed at load from the pinned fixture and PCA mechanics. A sideways drag or the left and right arrow keys spin the view, with the near side following the pointer as in the other figures; three buttons set the pitch, and clicking a pose loads its PCA coordinates; the sliders and buttons remain the keyboard path. `skill-static.svg` is the same renderer drawn into an SVG-recording context.
- Schematic sheets (`sheet.mjs`, top of the page): two drawn curved surfaces stacked above one flat map, with a single straight map line lifted onto both. It illustrates a shared chart, is not computed from the mechanics or fixtures, and is not one of the four panels. A reticle marks one place along the route on all three layers, joined by a dashed vertical, and two boxes beside the figure give its coordinates: six numbers for the two surface points, two for the map point. Each surface carries the map's grid stretched and bent sideways in its own way, so its outline runs outside the map's in places and inside it in others, no surface coordinate repeats a map coordinate, and the joining line leans. The boxes report the positions as drawn. The connection between layers is shown by the grid: the marked point's cell is tinted on all three, the two centre lines are drawn stronger on all three, and faint threads join matching corners. Pointing at any of the three lines moves the reticle, and it rests at the end of the route. The static page carries the default boxes, checked against `numbersAt` by the test. The pitch is fixed; dragging sideways or the left and right arrow keys spin the figure, with the near side following the pointer, and two sliders move the end of the route. The figure comes first in its panel, with the explanation beneath. `sheet-static.svg` is the same painter drawn into the SVG-recording context.
- Nested-spaces sketch (`nest.mjs`, grasp section): a wireframe box, a curved sheet inside it and a narrow band across the sheet, standing for the fourteen joint angles, the eight left by the grasp and the two a handoff uses. A sketch only, not computed from the mechanics or fixtures; a key in the corner gives the three counts. It spins like the other figures and has no other controls. `nest-static.svg` is the same painter drawn into the SVG-recording context.
- Number boxes (`numbers.mjs`): every robot panel writes its pose out beside the robot, with live values. The free-arms panel has a labelled box of the fourteen joint angles and, under it, the same angles typeset as the vector q. The other panels have no box: they show a formula for q as a function of the few numbers that produced the pose, and under it q written out again as `q = […]`. The formulas are `q = g(…)` for the eight grasp numbers, in the grasp panel and again in the cost panel, where the six the slice holds still are muted; and in the skill panel the decoder of the text, `q = f(…)` for the hand-designed Reach and Present (exact, through the solver) and `q ≈ f(…)` for the two fitted coordinates. The robot's right arm is always drawn behind the ball and its left arm in front. In the first two panels the boxes sit beside the robot; in the cost and skill panels, under the paired figures.
- Layout: in the cost and skill panels the robot sits beside its figure on wide screens and directly above it on narrow ones; `sideBySide` in `page.mjs` moves the scene at setup, so the no-JavaScript fallback keeps the plain stacked order. With the hand-designed controller the skill figure also dots in the Reach and Present lines, solved with the pinned IK, to show where the designed coordinates sit in the fitted space.
- Skill: PCA signs are arbitrary. The synthetic training data orients PC1 toward farther-forward ball positions and PC2 toward higher positions; these are mixed fitted axes, not Reach/Present or universal meanings. Score ranges come from the fixture, with an extended display range for the outside-family example. Hand-designed Reach/Present or training-fitted PC1/PC2, raw and projected residuals, held-out errors, and a deliberately out-of-family sideward target. Projection restores relative grasp geometry only; it need not recover the original demonstrated target.

All panels reset separately. No autoplay. Standard labelled controls support keyboard and touch. Distinct fixture-derived fallback diagrams and the full article survive disabled/failed JavaScript. Positions are metres; sliders show joint angles in degrees, internal mechanics use radians. Cost fields disclose torque's equal-load quasistatic assumptions and normalized proxy formulas. Original code/artwork is MIT-compatible; external sources are linked as further reading, not sources for synthetic numerical data.

Publishing remains a separate authorized action. Local commits and a valid pin are not evidence of a public release; do not claim source URLs resolve until the exact source revision is pushed.
